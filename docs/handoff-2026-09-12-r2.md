## 1a. Buying **up** — the row above costs too much to enter

**Extends §5, *Three constants and an index*.**

### The diagnosis

Two things you can buy at any moment: one more soul of the row you are on, or the
first soul of the row above. The row above produces **×5** (yield ×10, lives ×2 as
long) and costs **×10** the row's base. Your own row's price has been climbing 7% a
copy. So:

> **The row above becomes the better buy at copy `k`, where
> `ramp^k = costRatio ÷ rateRatio`.**

Live: `10 ÷ 5 = 2`, and `1.07^k = 2` at **k ≈ 10**.

| Cohort 1 copies owned | One more costs | **at ×10** (150) | **at ×7** (105) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 21 | 0.70× — worse | **1.00× — even** |
| **10** | **29** | **0.98× — even** | **1.40× — better** |
| 20 | 58 | 1.93× | 2.76× |

**Row two is the reported feel.** At ten copies the two buys are identical value —
but one is six seconds of saving and the other is thirty. **Equal value at five
times the wait reads as "too expensive to consider" even though the arithmetic says
it is a wash.**

### The reference figures

The ×10 is not the fault. Cookie Clicker runs 15 → 100 → 1,100 → 12,000 → 130,000 →
1.4M, roughly ×10 a tier; AdCap's Earth ladder runs 60 → 720 → 8,640 → 103,680, a
flat ×12. Both land the crossover at **k ≈ 5**, and both get there on the ramp —
Cookie Clicker's is ~1.15 throughout, AdCap's is per-business and **not monotonic**:
1.07 for the Lemonade Stand, then 1.15 · 1.14 · 1.13 · 1.12 · 1.11 · 1.10 · 1.09 ·
1.08, back to 1.07 for the Oil Company. **The first row is the cheap outlier and the
second is the steepest.** §5 adopted the outlier as the universal.

### The change: `costRatio` 10 → 7, ramp untouched

`1.07^k = 7/5 = 1.4` → **k ≈ 5**, which is Cookie Clicker's number. One constant.

> **No production figure moves.** Yield is still ×10 a row, lives are still ×2 as
> long, throughput is still ×5. **Only the ticket price changes.**

```
cost(n) = 15 × 7^(n−1)
```

| n | today | proposed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | 15 |
| 2 | 150 | **105** |
| 3 | 1,500 | 735 |
| 4 | 15,000 | 5,145 |
| 5 | 150,000 | 36,015 |
| 8 | 150M | 12.4M |

### ⚠ Two stated claims move with it

**Payback flattens.** `payback(n) = 30 s × 1.4^(n−1)`, not `× 2^(n−1)`:

| Cohort | at ×10 | at ×7 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 m | 42 s |
| 5 | 8 m | 1.9 m |
| 8 | 64 m | 5.3 m |

§5's *each cohort takes twice as long to repay itself as the one below it* becomes
×1.4. Later cohorts are still deliberately worse purchases at base, much less
punishingly so. **That is the direction the observed run wants** — it skipped depth
entirely and shot for height — but it is a change to a design claim and not just a
number.

**The reveal/clerk identity needs re-deriving.** §5's *a row appears at the exact
moment the row below it can be automated* holds because `revealMult × costRatio =
clerkMult`, and `25 × 10 = 250`. At ×7 that becomes **`25 × 7 = 175`**. It is
derived in code so it cannot drift — but the *argued* figure was 250×, justified as
sitting just past the row's own tier II, and **tier II is also moving** (§1b). The
identity survives automatically; the argument behind the number does not, and it
wants its own pass rather than a silent recompute.

### ⚠ Per-cohort ramp (r1's candidate B) is out, at the current gates

A steeper ramp buys **fewer** copies for the same spend. Cohort 2 reached 150 at
1.07; the same experience at 1.15 stops it near **78**. B pushes the gate ladder
further out of reach, and the gate ladder is already the problem. It comes back
below, but only coupled to §1b.

## 1b. Buying **deep** — the level ladder is inert, and this is why

**Extends §5, *Levels* and *Open*.**

Nothing here was in r1. It is the other half of the reported behaviour — not *the
next row is unaffordable* but *going deep didn't even feel efficient to try* — and
it turns out not to be a feeling.

### `priceFactor = 1` is the one value that makes a rung pointless

Compare a rung against one more copy at the same moment, at gate `g`, where `M` is
what one copy currently earns:

```
rung at gate g:   costs  g · c · ramp^g · pf     gains  g · M
one more copy:    costs      c · ramp^g          gains      M
```

The `g` cancels out of both sides. So:

> **A rung is exactly `1/priceFactor` times as efficient as one more copy, at every
> gate, on every cohort, forever.**

At `priceFactor = 1` they are dead even and there is never a reason to prefer one.
§5 calls `priceFactor` *the single pacing knob on every level in the game* and
leaves it unauthored; unauthored landed on the one value that switches the mechanic
off.

The useful framing falls straight out: **`priceFactor` means "how many times better
than one more copy a rung is."** `0.2` is five times better. That is a knob with an
intention behind it.

### The ladder's total is sized against nothing

Ten rungs of ×2 is **×1,024**, over a span where the ramp multiplies the price by
`1.07^400 ≈ 5.7 × 10¹¹`.

| Top gate | `1.07^G` | Ladder gives | Depth ends up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **400** (live) | 5.7 × 10¹¹ | ×1,024 | **5.5 × 10⁸ underwater** |
| 200 | 754,000 | ×1,024 | 736× underwater |
| **150** | 25,500 | ×1,024 | **25× underwater** |
| 100 | 868 | ×1,024 | 1.2× *ahead* — the row never prices out |

So §5's load-bearing claim — *a fully-levelled cohort 1 still earns while you are
buying cohort 8, and nothing else in the economy does that* — is out by roughly
four orders of magnitude at the live figures. It was never going to be true, and
the run correctly declined to find out.

### The rule: derive the top gate, do not type it

```
ramp^(top gate)  =  ladder total  ×  X
```

`X` is how far a maxed row's marginal buy should eventually fall behind its own
first copy — how much you want the row to price out in the end. At `ladder total =
1,024` and `X ≈ 25`:

| ramp | top gate |
|---|---|
| **1.07** | **150** |
| 1.08 | 132 |
| 1.10 | 106 |
| 1.12 | 90 |
| 1.15 | 73 |

**1.07 wants a top gate of 150, and the run stopped cohort 2 at exactly 150.** That
is the economy naming the number rather than the player getting bored, and it is
the strongest single piece of evidence in this document.

Scaling the live shape `[10, 25, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400]` to 150:

```
[5, 10, 20, 40, 55, 75, 95, 115, 130, 150]
```

⚠ **Gates first, `priceFactor` second, same commit.** Compressing the gates makes
×1,024 arrive far sooner, so a `priceFactor` fitted against gate 400 is overpowered
at 150. Author the gates from the rule, then set `priceFactor` against the
shortened ladder.

### The check the result has to pass

The whole point of depth is that a finished low row stays worth owning. At
`costRatio 7`, the 150-gate ladder and `priceFactor 0.2`:

| | Experience spent | Rate bought | Rate per 1M xp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cohort 1, 150 copies, all ten rungs | ~20.6M | 76,800/s | **3,730** |
| Cohort 8, first copy | 12.4M | 39,060/s | **3,160** |

Within 20% of each other. **That is §5's claim being true for the first time**, and
it is the acceptance test for whatever `priceFactor` finally lands on — if the two
columns diverge by more than about 2×, the knob is wrong.

### ⚠ And this is how the per-cohort ramp comes back

B failed because a steeper ramp met a fixed gate ladder. Under the rule above the
gates are **derived from the ramp**, so they move together:

```
ramp(1) = 1.07                              top gate 150
ramp(n) = max(1.07, 1.17 − 0.01n)   n ≥ 2   top gate from the rule
```

Cohort 2 at 1.15 tops out at 73 — near the 78 copies the same budget actually
reaches. Nothing is stranded. Gates become `topGate(ramp(n)) × fraction_i`, which
is **one formula and an index** rather than a second authored table, and §5's
direction since v3 survives.

⚠ **It still kills the single crossover.** §5's *the moment to move up the ladder
is the same moment at every rung, forever* is the section's central claim and this
ends it. Worth it, probably — but it is a rewrite of §5, not a footnote, and it
should not be attempted in the same pass as §1a.

## 2. Worlds want a pole

**New authored field in §13. Extends §14's alignment lock. Pays into §1's largest
hole.** *(Unchanged from r1.)*

A world declares a polarity it wants and pays for it. One field per world in a
table that already carries fifteen rows, and it is the first thing in the game that
**asks for a number other than zero**.

This supersedes the addendum's §E (`lightShare`) as the answer to world identity.
§E buys identity in cohort volatility, two steps from anything the player decides.
This buys it in the decision the game is about.

### ⚠ It cannot read the harvest snapshot

§14 reads alignment off excess at the instant of harvest — and `excessGate` means
excess at that instant is inside ±0.12 by construction, ±0.03 by world 5. **There
is no room there to record that a world wanted Burden and got it.** The gate and
the demand would be fighting over one reading.

Read a running quantity instead, accumulated over the stay:

```
matchShare = karma sent to the demanded pile while on this world
             ÷ total karma earned on this world
```

Then the two stop competing and start composing:

> **Tilt hard for most of the world to earn the demand. Spend the last stretch
> cleaning up to pass the gate and leave.** That is the arc the session found by
> accident, made standing.

### The payout — fold it into the lock, do not stack on it

A `demandBonus` multiplying §15's `perDelivery` alongside `anchorBonus` is a third
unbounded multiplier on a payout that already compounds across five worlds.
Instead, reprice §14's existing three-way trade so the world decides what its poles
are worth:

| Locked alignment | Today | With a demand `D` |
|---|---|---|
| the pole the world wants | E + K | **E + K × D** |
| the other pole | E + K | **E + K ÷ D** |
| Even | E × 3 | E × 3 (unchanged) |

Nothing new is added to the payout; **the same K is redistributed across the
poles.** `D` is bounded and authored, the three-way trade survives intact, and it
is the design's own idiom — §6's bias pair is mean-preserving for exactly this
reason. Start at `D = 2` and author `wants` per world.

⚠ Even is now the only lock a world cannot make better or worse. That may be
correct — Even is the *refusal* of the offer — but it interacts with §19's standing
*Even's experience bonus: ×1.5, ×2 or ×3* question and the two must be re-measured
together.

### Author the alternation, price it, do not gate it

`wants` across system 1: **− / + / − / + / −**. The swing is then forced
economically and never by a lock, which is §13's *the toll is a toll, not a
condition* one level up. A player who wants to eat the penalty and run one pole
forever still can; almost nobody will.

That lands the first-run-as-tutorial idea without a tutorial: the first system
teaches the swing by paying for it, and by the time prestige is behind you the
habit is built and the sandbox is whatever you make of it.

### ⚠ Three things it forces

- **The correction cost moves from once-a-run to once-a-world.** The addendum's §B
  measured clearing any tilt at ~10% of the run so far. Paid five times instead of
  once is a different number and has never been measured. **Do this before
  authoring §C's short-pile skim** — this may be all the teeth the axis needs, or
  it may make the skim unsurvivable.
- **The gate ladder is under pressure from two directions.** `excessGate` tightens
  0.12 → 0.03 while the demand asks for a deeper tilt on the later worlds. Check
  world 5 is passable at all. The addendum's §C flagged this retune arriving from
  the skim; it now arrives from here too, and it is one retune.
- **`reaimPhases` becomes load-bearing**, which is §3.

### Framing note

Not *karmic pollution*. A world that needs cleaning has a right answer, and §1 says
the game never moralises about the pole. The neutral shape is §12's, already
written: **the world offers and you may decline.** A world paying double for a
Burden life is an offer; locking Even for the experience is a legal answer.

## 3. Re-aim is priced by distance

**Replaces §6's flat `reaimPhases`.** *(Unchanged from r1.)*

The reported behaviour is re-aiming constantly and treating the penalty as
permanent background. A cost that is always on is not a cost.

### The rejection is spent

§6 rejected per-detent pricing because *a drag across the track paid the penalty
once per detent it crossed — the cost of a decision depended on how you happened to
make it.* **The confirm button answers that.** `|Δdetent|` is now well-defined at
the moment of confirm, whatever path the handle took. That objection no longer
applies and the section should say so.

### Distance, not destination

| Move | \|Δ\| | today | `\|Δ\|` | `ceil(\|Δ\|/2)` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 → +1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 0 → +2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| +1 → −1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| +2 → −1 | 3 | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| +2 → −2 | 4 | 1 | 4 | 2 |

⚠ **Do not price by destination.** Making ±2 expensive to *enter* is thematically
right and mechanically wrong: §6 states that hard aim with the refinery idled is
the only correction fast enough to clear a deep tilt. That is the emergency brake,
and under §2 it gets pulled once a world.

⚠ **World 1 is eight phases total.** `|Δ|` puts a full swing at half the first
world. `ceil(|Δ|/2)` is the safe authoring; `|Δ|` is the honest one and probably
wants §19's standing question — *whether `reaimPhases` should be a share of the
world* — resolved first.

### Keep the cost in duration, not depth

The end-of-penalty marker on the wave strip is the readout that made this feel
deliberate. **A variable duration moves that marker; a variable depth is
invisible.** So scale `reaimPhases` and leave `reaimPenalty: 0.65` alone.

### ⚠ Doc drift to resolve in the same pass

**§6 says `reaimPhases: 1`. §13 and §19 both argue from `reaimPhases: 2`.** One is
wrong and neither knows it. Check `balance.ts` and correct the loser before
retuning.

  So the transcription debt travelled with steps 1–2 rather than after them.
- **`priceFactor: 0.2` needed no sim pass.** Run against the real `buildings.ts`

  a 2× bar.
- **`COHORT_DECADE` had to split in two.** *No production figure moves* means
  `COST_DECADE = 7` and `YIELD_DECADE = 10`; one constant carried both.
- **Step 4 resolved to keeping `revealFactor: 25`.** The clerk is 175 × cost(n)
  by derivation, which is ~38 copies of the row against 43 before — the same
  place. The *tier II* anchor is dead and retired, because a rung's price is now
  a function of `priceFactor` and cannot anchor anything.
- **Step 5 resolved to 1.** Code and §6 agreed; §13, §19 and `progression.md`
  were the drift. They argued *two phases is a quarter of world 1*, which is now
  true as written — that is what a full swing costs under step 6.

### Step 7 landed, and what it cost

`design.md` §13 and §14 carry the demand; `progression.md` carries the argument.

- **`matchShare` is two sums, not a ratio.** `Planet` keeps `demandedKarma` and
  `earnedKarma` — a world is saved mid-stay far more often than at the harvest,
  and a ratio cannot be added to. `demandBonus` locks at departure beside the
  anchors'. Save is **v6**; a v5 world takes 0 / 0 / 1, since nothing can invent
  a share that was never counted.
- **`resolveHarvestYields`' last two arguments became one options object.** Four
  positionals with two defaults was already at its limit and the demand made a
  fifth. `{ anchorBonus, demandBonus }`; the three-argument calls are unchanged.
- **The anchor bonus multiplies everything, the demand multiplies karma alone.**
  That is the whole difference between them in code.
- **Granting karma in DevPanel does not move the demand**, because the world
  tallies what the cohorts *earned* on it. The panel got its own verb — two
  buttons, its way and the other — or the axis takes a full world to watch.
- **`D` is authorable per world and authored nowhere.** `balance.harvest.demand`
  is 2 and every world takes it; `PlanetDemand.factor` is the override.
- **The demand reads production, not holdings**, so the refinery's intake never
  moves it. Worth keeping true — it is what stops the two systems coupling.

**Unseen, in the order it wants an eye:** the Aim panel's aside, which now reads
*Wants Burden — 62% served, harvest karma ×1.3* whenever the world asks for a
pole and nothing is owed; then `AlignmentPanel`'s second note line on the harvest
screen, which says the same in the past tense; then `OutputPanel`'s yields, which
now carry the demand and did not before.

**Step 8 is the blocker it always was** — the per-world correction cost is still
unmeasured, and nothing above changes that.

### §2's one open question was decided before step 7 was taken

**`D` reads `matchShare`, continuously:**

```
K × D^(2 · matchShare − 1)
```

Full service to the demanded pole pays `D`, half pays 1, full opposition pays
`1/D`. Mean-preserving in log space, which is §6's bias pair's own idiom.

The three-row table in §2 above is **the poles of that curve, not three cases.**
Read literally — `D` keyed on the locked alignment — the arc §2 argues for does
not work: cleaning up to pass the gate lands you in `evenBand` and wipes the
demand you spent the world earning. Keyed on `matchShare` the demand is banked as
you go and the cleanup costs nothing, which is what *tilt hard for most of the
world, spend the last stretch cleaning up* actually describes.

`matchShare` is entirely new state — `PlanetSnapshot` carries no per-world karma
accumulator. The feed is `Building.#payKarma`, which already has the
positive/negative split in hand.

### Step 9 landed, and §1b had the wrong half of it

`design.md` §5 carries the result; `progression.md` §*A level has no price*
carries the argument.

- **The derived gates were avoidable.** §1b accepted a gate ladder per cohort as
  the price of a per-cohort ramp. The binding constraint was the *rung price* —
  delete it and the solvency rule goes with it, so the ladder stays shared.
- **A priced rung was never a decision.** §1b's own arithmetic shows it: the gate
  cancels, so a rung is worth a fixed multiple of one more copy everywhere. At
  `priceFactor 1` it is off and at anything lower it dominates on sight. §1b read
  that as *author the multiple*; it reads better as *there is no multiple to
  author*. `priceFactor` is retired, not retuned.
- **The gates are AdCap's, authored as authored** — `[25, 50, 100, 200, 300,
  400]`, six rungs, shared by every cohort. Free rungs are what made that legal:
  with no price, nothing can be stranded above a steep row's reach.
- **Nothing was built.** `acquireUnpriced` and `releaseUnheld` already did the
  whole mechanic. Deleting `costs` from `levelUpgrades` was the code change.

⚠ **The depth axis does not pay at these gates, and §5 now says so.** Six rungs
is ×64 against the packed ten-rung draft's ×1_024, spread over four times the
span, so a levelled cohort 1 crosses *below* cohort 8's first copy at about a
hundred copies and never returns. The old claim that a fully-levelled low row
still earns late is retired, not restated. **The cheapest fix is the rung, not
the ladder** — six rungs of ×4 is ×4,096 and restores the crossing, at the cost
of *a rung halves the life*, which only cohorts 1 and 2 can honour at six rungs
anyway.

⚠ **Two smaller things go with it.** Gate 400 costs 121 trillion experience and
is never reached, so the ladder has a terminal rung nobody takes. And only
cohorts 1 and 2 reach the 62.5 ms floor now; cohort 8 bottoms out at four
seconds a life.

⚠ **Still unrun.** Nothing above has been through the bench, and the buy policy's
shape changed qualitatively — watch where `payback` stops on the hundred-copy
run-up to gate 200.
